# Additional early opportunity sources

Researched October 4, 2026. Companion to
[the initial program-source proposal](PROGRAM_SOURCE_CANDIDATES.md). This document
records public source facts and proposed coverage. It contains no applicant
profile or personal eligibility decisions. No sources were enabled, production
records ingested, or application/registration actions taken. Changes remain local.

The early-opportunities feed should include dedicated first-/second-year
programs, beginner-accessible building events, research entry routes, and relevant
career/technical events. A program need not use the word "freshman" to be useful.
Conversely, "early careers" does not override an explicit graduation restriction.
An undated overview belongs in monitoring, rather than being presented as an open
application. The institution-specific sources below should be optional coverage
packs; attending that institution is a source condition, not a repository default.

## Recommended source expansion order

1. Add readable first-/second-year discovery families: Capital One's scoped
   Analyst/Product/Tech Summits, Two Sigma New Seekers and BCG Launch. Carry forward
   the initial proposal's Jane Street/Citadel/HRT sources. These broaden early
   discovery while keeping missing cycle dates/funding visible.
2. Add dated beginner building events: TreeHacks and LA Hacks, plus the fall AI
   event only after its rules are established. Treat hackathons as their own
   category rather than forcing them into internships.
3. Add Caltech SURF and optional UCLA research/founder coverage. Separate paid
   incubators, free workshops and research stipends; long programs still use the
   ordinary location policy.
4. Add OFC career/exhibition and other California technical-event coverage with
   pass-specific costs. Keep distant paid conferences conditional on funding or
   a privately configured budget; short duration alone does not imply funded
   travel. Monitor Dorm Room Fund for a future student-investor cycle.
5. Add restricted sophomore cycles, conference grants and international variants
   with exact rules. Defer stale pages and JavaScript shells until their content
   and current-cycle status can be reliably collected.

This ordering is a coverage proposal, not a personal ranking. The private workflow
should apply the selected academic stage, graduation window, institution,
geography, interests and cost/travel preferences. Missing GPA, research placement,
funding and coursework remain explicit questions. An old cycle stays recoverable
in monitoring but should not occupy the actionable board as an open opportunity.

## Extraction and validation implications

- Retain full scoped requirements before shortening display text. Include
  qualifications in public `JobPosting` JSON-LD when present, with provenance.
  Susquehanna Growth Equity is an example of richer structured data than visible
  text. HTTP 200 and a title alone do not constitute a valid extraction.
- Preserve source distinctions between stage and graduation dates, annual
  windows and specific cycles, open applications and notification routes, free
  admission and capped travel, paid employment and investments. Do not inherit a
  sibling program's requirements or benefits.
- Conference records need access scope, ticket price/currency, age restrictions,
  travel/housing coverage and its conditions. Community programs need workload,
  fees and exclusivity obligations alongside subject/category facts.
- Keep raw conflicting statements with page/section provenance. Native Bain body
  text omits some dates; UCLA URFP has conflicting paid-research rules; DesignCon
  and NSBE mix cycle years. Extraction should expose these for private review
  rather than resolve them by an unsupported guess.
- Institution coverage is configurable public source selection. Personal choices
  and eligibility decisions remain in the external private workspace. No model
  dependency is needed in the collector; external AI can research bounded
  unresolved fields and interpret preferences in the private workflow.

The native collector fetcher probed 36 exact public URLs with robots checks,
normal throttle, bounded responses, three workers and normal redirect checks.
Thirty-five returned HTTP 200; Tech Week returned HTTP 429 and was not retried
outside the fetcher's normal behavior. Several successful responses were
incomplete: Contrary had no usable text, Palantir had a shell, the Susquehanna
directory lacked listings, and the LA AI event lacked full rules. Public JSON-LD
was subsequently inspected offline for Susquehanna Growth Equity. Other links
were researched through official web pages/indexing; not every linked page was
included in the native probe.

Ignored evidence is under `data/source-research/20261004/extended/`. It is research
